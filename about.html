What I am after

I want robots that get better without a human correcting every mistake. Imitation learning only teaches a policy the states a demonstrator happened to visit, so every additional nine of reliability costs another order of magnitude of teleoperation. The way out is a flywheel: systems that close their own loop, notice when they have drifted off distribution, and turn their own failures into the next batch of training data. My organizing idea is Acting Fast and Slow. A reactive policy is fast, cheap and confidently wrong off distribution; search is slow, expensive and able to recover. Nearly everything I build puts both in one loop and spends compute only where the fast system is about to fail.

Background

M.S. in Robotic Systems Development from the CMU Robotics Institute (GPA 4.08/4.0), and a dual degree in Mechanical Engineering and Systems Design with a Computer Science minor from IIT Kharagpur (GPA 9.22/10), where I received the Institute Order of Merit, given to roughly 8 of more than 1500 graduating students. SAILOR was a Spotlight at NeurIPS 2025, roughly the top 3 percent of submissions, and I have four other peer-reviewed papers across robotics and vision, plus an arXiv preprint. Before Nuro I worked on prediction at Plus AI and motion planning at Aurora. Along the way I built a fair number of machines: a terrace farming robot that climbs 40 cm steps, an autonomous lunar excavator, a ground vehicle fabricated from scratch that placed twice at IGVC, and a 1/10 scale racecar that learned to drive inside a model of the world.

Right now

I am a research scientist on the Behavior Planning team at Nuro, post-training flow-based driving policies with reinforcement learning so the vehicle drives the way a person would. Outside of that I am exploring world action models: the bet that the robotics GPT moment comes from models that predict what the world does next and what to do about it in one representation, rather than from ever larger piles of teleoperation. I am also building on an SO101 arm at home, mostly to keep my own hands on the data collection and evaluation loop rather than reasoning about it from a paper.
