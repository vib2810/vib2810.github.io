Planning for berm construction

Energy-optimal excavation sequencing

Fall 2023 · CMU · Planning for Robotics (16-782)
Course project
Field robotics Planning & control

The problem

Building a berm is not one excavation, it is dozens, and the order matters more than any individual pass. Each pass changes the terrain the next one starts from, and driving between distant excavation sites burns energy that a lunar platform does not have. Optimizing site assignment and pass trajectories jointly is intractable at any useful worksite size.

What I built

  • Formulated berm construction as a bilevel problem: the outer level assigns and sequences excavation sites, the inner level plans the trajectory for each pass.
  • Optimized total operation energy, excavation plus the driving between sites, subject to worksite and reachability constraints.
  • Implemented in C++ on ROS 2 as a task optimizer node, with worksites specified in YAML and the planned sequence rendered as an animation for inspection.
  • Evaluated on three test environments of increasing size against greedy nearest-site baselines.
Fed into LunAR-X
The sequencing formulation carried directly into the MRSD capstone excavation system, which went on to build straight and curved berms autonomously on a regolith simulant testbed.

What worked

  • Ordering matters more than site selection on its own. Because each pass changes the terrain the next one starts on, a greedy nearest-site choice looks cheap locally and compounds into long transits late in the build.
  • The bilevel split is what makes it tractable. Once the site assignment is fixed, each inner trajectory problem is small and independent, so the outer search can afford to evaluate many assignments.

What didn't

  • The formulation assumes the terrain updates predictably after each pass. Real excavation does not deposit exactly what the model predicts, and the planner has no closed loop against a measured berm profile.
  • This is a course project result: the comparison is against greedy baselines in simulation, with no hardware measurement of the energy savings it claims.
